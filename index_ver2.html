garch-risk-analytics

Repository
  • A tested, installable Python package combining the following systems:
  • Rolling GJR-GARCH volatility forecasts feeding Monte-Carlo VaR and Expected Shortfall with Kupiec and Christoffersen backtests
  • Option Greeks, and a full price/volatility stress framework
  • Built to be honest about where the models break and be able to assess them robustly

Results

  • GARCH-fitted degrees of freedom cleanly separate near-Gaussian equities from genuinely fat-tailed Bitcoin
  • Bitcoin VaR passes Kupiec coverage at both 95% and 99%, with breach independence holding across all assets
  • GARCH volatility beats a naive rolling-window forecast on QLIKE for every asset

Monte Carlo & ML Methods for VaR & ES

Paper
  • A comparison of traditional and machine-learning approaches to tail-risk estimation across equities and Bitcoin
  • Parametric GBM and historical bootstrap set against GAN and deep-quantile-regression scenario generators
  • Validated with unconditional- and conditional-coverage backtests
  • Co-authored.

Results

  • Traditional Monte-Carlo methods were strongest on Bitcoin and weakest on NASDAQ, where fatter tails and volatility clustering break their assumptions
  • GBM and bootstrap both pass Kupiec coverage on Bitcoin but fail Christoffersen on the equity indices, exposing volatility underestimation in stress
  • The GAN and DQR models proved far too conservative, with breach rates of 24 to 40% against a 5% target

Mathematical Model of a Pregnancy Test

Paper
  • An Indian Academy of Sciences summer research fellowship
  • A diffusion PDE with a nonlinear reaction boundary condition
  • Reduced via Laplace transform and convolution to a nonlinear Volterra integro-differential equation
  • Solved numerically with a product-integration and fixed-point iteration scheme in MATLAB

Results

  • In a diffusion-limited, antigen-favoured regime the product concentration stabilises near 0.7 and the surface antigen near 0.6
  • Raising the molar ratio so the antibody dominates drives both concentrations lower, matching the expected limiting-reactant behaviour
